What Is Cheese, Really?

Cheese begins when milk separates into curds and whey. The curds are pressed, salted, and aged. Every variable matters: the animal, the season, the cave, the patience of the maker. Change one and you get a different cheese. This is why a single recipe has produced thousands of distinct varieties.

The word cheese comes from the Latin caseus, which also gives us the word "casein," the main milk protein.

Famous Families of Cheese

Fresh Cheeses
Fresh cheeses are young and mild. They include mozzarella, ricotta, and chevre. Eat these within days.
Soft-Ripened Cheeses
Soft-ripened cheeses grow a white bloomy rind. Brie and Camembert are the classic examples. The rind is edible and you should eat it.
Blue Cheeses
Blue cheeses are veined with Penicillium roqueforti. Roquefort, Gorgonzola, and Stilton lead this family. They are salty, sharp, and divisive.
Hard Cheeses
Hard cheeses are aged for months or years. Parmigiano-Reggiano, aged Gouda, and Comte reward the wait with crystals of crunch and deep, nutty flavor.

A Word of Caution

Warning: The American product known as "processed cheese food" is, at best, distantly related to cheese. Read the label.
